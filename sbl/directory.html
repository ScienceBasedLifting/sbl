What this group teaches you

This is where the "why" lives. Jewers, TNF, and Paul Carter argue that only the final reps near muscular failure drive growth — everything before is junk volume. They favor machines over barbells because stability lets the target muscle reach failure without stabilizer exhaustion. Kassem Hanson and Joe Bennett add individualization: your anatomy determines which exercises are optimal for you.

Jeff Nippard and Milo Wolf provide the academic counterweight. Nippard blends literature reviews with practical programming. Wolf's ROM research clarifies that mechanical tension at long muscle lengths drives growth — not "stretch" itself, a distinction the influencer ecosystem frequently oversimplifies. Ben Yanes bridges both camps, warning against the jargon overload that leads to paralysis by analysis.

Nutrition / energy balance

Scientific Snitch

Ella Parungao breaks down nutrition debates, energy balance, main-gaining, and research interpretation at the cellular level.

Full profile

Background: Researcher and evidence-based communicator. Screen-shares PubMed studies with clinical breakdown graphics.

Core philosophy: Dissects cellular nutrition (lipogenesis vs. hyperplasia). Highly critical of the traditional "bulking" paradigm, advocating for main-gaining and precise energy balance for natural lifters.

Platform: Co-hosts "The Mind Muscle Connection" podcast, Patreon community, debate panels.

Instagram TikTok Links

Why nutrition gets its own section

Most fitness content treats nutrition as an afterthought — "just eat in a surplus." Ella Parungao has disrupted that by showing that lipogenesis and muscular hypertrophy operate on distinct energetic pathways. For natural lifters without exogenous androgens altering metabolic partitioning, massive caloric surpluses frequently result in rapid fat accumulation without accelerating muscle protein synthesis. Her work argues for conservative surpluses and more careful energy balance thinking.

When general advice is not enough

Roman Mori applies the 80/20 principle to fighters — heavy progressive overload builds durability on the mat without the neurological fatigue that comes from overly "functional" training. Juan Samudio uses his engineering background to hyper-specialize in gluteal development, mapping exact resistance curves to bias specific muscle regions.

Jake Doleschal proves that the best "new" ideas are sometimes very old ones: Bronze and Silver era bodybuilders naturally gravitated toward high-frequency, low-volume full-body training before steroids changed the equation. His programming stimulates muscles 3-4 times per week with restricted volume per session, aligning perfectly with modern data on Muscle Protein Synthesis windows.

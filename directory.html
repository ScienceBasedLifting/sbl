Exercise Selection

Why we pick certain exercises over others.

If your goal is maximum hypertrophy, you prioritize exercises based on the stimulus they give to specific muscles — resistance profiles, stretch-mediated tension, and biomechanics-driven selection. If your goal is time efficiency, you pick general compound movements that hit multiple muscles in a 45-minute session. This section covers both approaches.

Why wellness gets its own section

Most fitness content treats nutrition as an afterthought — "just eat in a surplus." Scientific Snitch shows that lipogenesis and hypertrophy operate on distinct energetic pathways, arguing for conservative surpluses over aggressive bulking. John Jewett brings the rare combination of IFBB Pro competition and a registered dietitian credential. Eric Trexler provides the deepest academic grounding through published research on energy availability and metabolic adaptation.

Layne Norton's PhD work on protein metabolism and leucine thresholds has genuinely shaped evidence-based nutrition practices, even if his combative delivery style divides opinion. Together, these creators cover nutrition from beginner-accessible explainers through to primary-research-level depth.

Physiology & Biomechanics

The deep science — muscle physiology, anatomy, and biomechanics.

This is the hardest section to explain and the most rewarding to learn. Muscle architecture, motor-unit recruitment, mechanical tension theory, tendon biomechanics, lengthened partials, and individualized exercise selection by anthropometry. These creators go deep. You don't need this section to build a great physique — but if you want to understand why training works at a cellular level, start here.

What do the tier badges mean?

Tier 1 Established creators with strong credentials, large audiences, and reliable accuracy. These are the primary recommendations.

Tier 2 Solid creators with good content and growing audiences. Accurate but may have narrower scope or less consistent output than Tier 1.

Community Smaller creators who reliably translate established science into accessible formats. Worth following but not primary educators.

Inspiration Pro-level competitors who train with evidence-based methods. Impressive to watch, but their volume and recovery prescriptions don't transfer to most natural lifters. See the inspiration page.

Two ways to pick exercises

Bennett coaches IFBB pros on execution — if your exercises don't feel right, he's the fix. Yanes uses whiteboard explanations and skeletal models to teach resistance profiles without jargon. King Deltoids goes deep on shoulder isolation with torque-profile analysis for each delt head.

Bankston and Dxklan represent the other end: fewer exercises, maximum effort per set, programming built around execution quality.

Why wellness gets its own section

Most lifting content treats nutrition as "eat in a surplus, bro." Scientific Snitch argues against aggressive bulking with PubMed breakdowns of lipogenesis vs. hyperplasia. Jewett is that rare case — an IFBB Pro who's also a Registered Dietitian with an MS in Nutrition, so his contest prep advice has clinical backing. Norton's PhD work on protein metabolism and leucine thresholds has genuinely shaped evidence-based nutrition practices. His delivery is combative, but his published research stands on its own. Between these four, you get nutrition covered from beginner-accessible to primary-research depth.
